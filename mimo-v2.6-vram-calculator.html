Reading the results like a pro

  • The official checkpoint is already quantized: 566GB for 1.02T params (~0.553 B/param) — experts in FP8, attention in BF16. Rebuilding naive FP8 (1,024GB) or BF16 (2,048GB) buys headroom, not a new tier of hardware.
  • MoE economics: Pro activates only 42B of 1,024B params per token (Flash 15B of 311B) — tokens/s runs like a mid-size dense model, but VRAM must hold every expert. The 9B distill is the one tier that behaves like a normal local model.
  • 1M-token context is the hidden cost: the default 16GB KV allowance is a short-context figure; long-context serving multiplies KV needs. Budget accordingly before buying hardware.
  • 9B is the consumer tier: Qwen3.5-9B base, MIT license, 18.8GB BF16 → single RTX 4090/5090; Q4 GGUF (~5.5GB) opens 16GB cards.

FAQ

How much VRAM does MiMo V2.6 Pro need?

The official FP8 checkpoint is 566GB of weights — ~627GB total with overhead and KV cache. That's 8× H100 80GB (640GB, just barely), 8× H200, or 2× Mac Studio 512GB. A full BF16 copy is 2,048GB.

Can MiMo V2.6 Flash run on a Mac Studio 512GB?

Yes — the official checkpoint needs ~203GB total, fitting a single 512GB Mac Studio or even 4× H100 80GB pooled. Flash is the sweet spot for serious local deployment.

Can MiMo V2.6 Distill 9B run on one consumer GPU?

Yes — 18.8GB BF16 (~22.3GB total) fits an RTX 4090 24GB tightly or an RTX 5090 32GB comfortably; a Q4 quant (~5.5GB weights) runs on 16GB cards.

Does the official checkpoint lose quality vs BF16?

FP8 expert weights + BF16 attention is the precision Xiaomi itself ships and serves; treat it as the reference quality. Only rebuild BF16 if you need to fine-tune or audit at full precision.
